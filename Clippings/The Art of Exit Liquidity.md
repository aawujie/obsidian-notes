You might have noticed after inflating private companies to the trillions, the VCs are finally ready to sell. The only problem is that they need some exit liquidity.

To be clear: I am not accusing the San Francisco venture complex of doing anything illegal. I am accusing them of doing something deeply immoral and destructive to the social promises of capitalism.

## The Bargain

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HGiRIezWgAAxezL?format=png&name=large)

the boomers were the last ones to collect these bad boys

America doesn't have a European welfare state. It was never supposed to need one. The deal was that the stock market would be the welfare state. Defined benefit gave way to defined contribution. Pensions gave way to 401ks. Social security became a floor nobody planned to live on. The implicit replacement was that every worker would become a shareholder, and the elevator going up for capital would carry labor up with it. Wages could stall and inequality could widen because the retirement account was compounding in the background and everyone was on the same ride, it would work out well enough in the end.

This is what made American inequality politically survivable. You could accept the boss made four hundred times your wage as long as your 401k was riding the same curve as the boss's. The passive index fund was the purest expression of the deal. The cashier, the teacher, the plumber, all free-riding on the price discovery done by sophisticated capital, capturing the market return, going fishing. The market was a commons.

The bargain requires conditions. Public markets must to be the venue where value actually is created. Access to the upside must be broad. The marginal dollar of capital formation has to be a dollar the index can own. These conditions held for a long time. They don't hold anymore.

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HGiRoMdXYAAID4M?format=jpg&name=large)

this is what they stole from you

When companies stay private until they are worth a trillion dollars, public markets are no longer where value is created. They are where value is realized. What happens in the public market now is the distribution, not the compounding. Every percentage point of return that used to accrue to passive retirement capital during a company's growth phase now accrues to whoever was already on the cap table before the round that priced the company at two hundred billion dollars. Figma went public and traded down fifty percent from its private mark within weeks. Klarna dropped ninety. Unfortunately, the system is working as designed.

The industry has noticed that this leaves retail behind, and the industry has a solution, which is to offer retail the ability to participate in the private markets too. This is the pitch. Democratization. Access. Closing the gap. What's actually being offered is the right to buy, at the top of a decade-long private-market expansion, positions that insiders accumulated when the same companies were worth a thousandth of what they are today. USVC is not access. It is a distribution mechanism for positions that have already run. Naval's own pitch admits this.

## Engineering an Exit

As always, crypto was the first to figure out how to scam you.

The playbook emerged when foundations realized they were sitting on enormous treasuries of their own locked tokens and native retail demand had dried up. The unlock cliffs were approaching and there was nobody to sell to. The solution was to wrap the locked bags in equity vehicles that regulated TradFi buyers could access. The token, which retail would never have bought directly, became a stock, which institutions could buy through compliant channels and retail could buy through their brokerage. The bag got distributed. The SEC did nothing. The foundations got out. The equity buyers got a position whose underlying asset was designed to be sold to them.

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HGiR0JRWUAAs1Q1?format=png&name=large)

Naval was early to crypto too btw

The SF venture complex watched this work and decided a trillion-dollar version was available. USVC is one door. The Nasdaq rule change is another. Nasdaq is proposing that newly listed companies with low public float get index weights calibrated to five times their float, capped at full weighting, with the math updated at each quarterly rebalance. For a company (SpaceX) IPOing with five percent float at a $1.75 trillion valuation, this means passive funds are forced to buy it as though it were a $438 billion position, fifteen days after listing, with no seasoning. The lock-up can be timed to expire into the next rebalance, at which point the mechanical weighting upgrades to full and passive funds are forced to aggressively buy the stock at the exact moment insiders are legally able to sell it. SpaceX is targeting mid-June. The rebalance is in December. The math works.

The index fund, which was supposed to be retail's protection against insider games, has been turned into the mechanism by which insider games are resolved. Your 401k is being taken for a ride.

The pattern is the same in both cases. Insiders accumulate positions in a venue that retail cannot access. The positions mature. Organic demand in the native venue proves insufficient to absorb the supply at the desired price. A wrapper is engineered that makes the position accessible to a different class of capital, typically through retirement accounts or passive flows, which buy price-insensitively because they are rule-bound rather than discretionary. The insiders get out. The new buyers hold the bag. The mechanism is legal because the wrapper is structured to be legal. The SEC is asleep at the wheel because crime is legal.

## The Consequences

This is partly why Sam Altman is being firebombed, Waymos are being attacked, and data centers are being protested. The people lighting things on fire do not have a theory of exit liquidity. They don't need one. They can see, in their own lives, that there is a class of people who were early and a class of people who are late, and the gap between those classes is widening faster than any narrative the late class can tell itself about meritocracy or timing or hard work. The technocratic class is producing visible, ambient evidence that ordinary capital is being farmed to realize extraordinary gains for people who are already extraordinary.

The K-shape is going to sharpen. What comes next is not a correction. Corrections happen inside systems whose participants still believe in the rules. The firebomb is just politics.
